# Gabarit for WinUI 3 (Windows)

Gabarit's look for WinUI 3, from SkollN's design (the reference), as XAML resources: they serve an app written in
Swift (swift-winrt) as well as one in C#.

| File                       | What                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Resources/Gabarit.xaml`   | Generated by `tools/tokens.py`: the colours by role (light and dark), the palette, radii, spacing, text styles, and WinUI's own control resources in Gabarit's colours (ToggleSwitch, TextBox, ComboBox, Slider, ListView, the accent). |
| `Resources/Controls.xaml`  | Gabarit's buttons (`GButtonPrimaryStyle`, `…Secondary…`, `…Ghost…`, `…Danger…`, `…Destructive…`, `…Regular…`), the close cross, the windows' dark header, the rail, cards, rows, badges, passing messages. It merges `Gabarit.xaml`. |
| `Resources/Icons.xaml`     | Generated by `tools/tokens.py`: Phosphor's fonts (`GIconFont`, `GIconFillFont`), a `FontIcon`'s styles (`GIconStyle`, `GIconFillStyle`) and each SF Symbol's character (`GIcon_<name>`, the dots as `_`). Merged by `Controls.xaml`. |
| `Resources/Fonts`          | IBM Plex (SIL Open Font License), Phosphor's two fonts (MIT).                                                  |

Copy `Resources` into the app as `Gabarit/` (content files), and merge `Controls.xaml` after WinUI's own resources:

```xml
<ResourceDictionary.MergedDictionaries>
    <XamlControlsResources xmlns="using:Microsoft.UI.Xaml.Controls"/>
    <ResourceDictionary Source="ms-appx:///Gabarit/Controls.xaml"/>
</ResourceDictionary.MergedDictionaries>
```

The windows, as on the Mac (`GWindow`, `GAppShell`): extend the content into the title bar
(`AppWindow.TitleBar.ExtendsContentIntoTitleBar = true`), put a `Grid` with `GWindowHeaderStyle` on top (52 high, dark,
room for the system's caption buttons and their Snap Layouts on the right) and give it to `Window.SetTitleBar`. The
main window: that header as its top band, a `StackPanel` with `GRailStyle` on the left, and the page in a `Border` with
`GShellPageStyle` (its rounded top-left corner). Mica or Acrylic can show behind the frame; Gabarit stays flat
everywhere else.

- The frame's elements (header, rail) take `RequestedTheme="Dark"` on the element itself; the header's right padding
  comes from `AppWindow.TitleBar.RightInset` (and `LeftInset`), with `PreferredHeightOption = Tall` for 52.
- The rail's pages are `RadioButton`s with `GRailItemStyle`, the same `GroupName`, `IsChecked` on the current page
  (screen readers say it); a count on the icon goes in `AutomationProperties.ItemStatus`.
- The window's root element takes `FontFamily="{StaticResource GFontText}"`: a `TextBlock` outside a control would
  otherwise be in Segoe UI. Each weight of IBM Plex is its own key (`GFontTextMedium`, `GFontTextSemibold`): WinUI
  would draw a false bold from the regular file.
- The close cross takes `<KeyboardAccelerator Key="Escape"/>`; an app not in English sets its
  `AutomationProperties.Name` and `ToolTipService.ToolTip`.
- Controls beyond WinUI's own: chips and underlined tabs are `RadioButton`s with `GChipStyle` and `GNavTabStyle`; a
  switch row is a `ToggleSwitch` with `GSwitchStyle` beside its title and `GSwitchDetailStyle`; badges take
  `GBadgeStyle` or a state's (`GBadgeSuccessStyle`…, with their text styles); a field's title `GFieldTitleStyle`, a
  section's `GSectionTitleStyle`, a page's sentence `GPageSummaryStyle`, a passing message's text `GToastTextStyle`.
  WinUI's own fields, menus and switches take body text (13) and fields are 30 high, as on the Mac.
- High contrast: every Gabarit colour, the frame's too, has a `HighContrast` value from the system's colours, so the
  pages follow Windows' contrast themes; WinUI's own controls keep their own contrast look.

The icons: the apps write the Mac's names (SF Symbols), as everywhere; WinUI draws Phosphor's icon, the one GTK draws.
In XAML, `<FontIcon Style="{StaticResource GIconStyle}" Glyph="{StaticResource GIcon_bell}"/>` (the dots become
`_`: `GIcon_arrow_triangle_2_circlepath`), and for a filled one (`lock.fill`) `GIconFillStyle` with the name without
`.fill`. In Swift, `GSymbols.glyph(for: "lock.fill")` gives the character and the font. A `FontSize` of the Mac's size × 1.25: Phosphor leaves more room around its drawings.

The pointer: Windows keeps the arrow over buttons (its own convention); an app that wants the pointing hand sets
`ProtectedCursor` in code, XAML cannot.

Not yet checked on Windows: `tools/xaml_check.py` (CI) checks that every reference resolves, in order, that the theme
dictionaries agree and that the keys and fonts are right, but these resources still have to be tried in a WinUI 3 app
on the Windows PC.
